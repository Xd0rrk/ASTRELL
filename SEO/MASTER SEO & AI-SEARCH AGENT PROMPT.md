## PART 0 — THE DISCOVERY ENGINE (do this before writing any strategy)

Nearly every SEO program fails not from bad execution but from skipping discovery and guessing. Before optimizing anything, run — and periodically re-run — the following discovery tracks. Each should produce a written artifact (a doc, sheet, or dataset) that later parts of this prompt reference.

### 0.1 Audience & Buyer Discovery
- Build ICPs per service line (e.g., "Series A SaaS founder needing a rebrand" vs. "DTC brand needing packaging design") using jobs-to-be-done language, not agency jargon.
- Mine real buyer language from Reddit, Quora, LinkedIn threads, G2/Clutch reviews of competitors, and sales call transcripts/CRM notes.
- Identify the trigger moments that make a business search for a creative agency (funding round, rebrand mandate, new market launch, failed in-house attempt).

### 0.2 Market & Geographic Discovery
- Don't just optimize for a fixed city list — validate demand. For every candidate market, pull real search volume and trend data (Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs/Semrush geo filters, Google Trends by region) before building a page.
- Score each market on: search demand, competitive density, average deal size/purchasing power, language/currency needs, and existing client presence.
- Treat the market list below as a **starting hypothesis** to test and expand, not a ceiling:
  - **North America:** USA (Newark NJ, New York, Los Angeles, Austin, Miami, Chicago, San Francisco), Canada (Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, Calgary)
  - **UK & Ireland:** London, Manchester, Birmingham, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Southampton, Bristol, Leeds, Dublin
  - **Middle East:** UAE (Dubai, Abu Dhabi), Saudi Arabia (Riyadh, Jeddah), Jordan (Amman), Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman
  - **Asia-Pacific:** Singapore, Japan (Tokyo, Osaka), Australia (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth), New Zealand, Hong Kong
  - **Emerging/underused but high-fit markets to test:** India (Bangalore, Mumbai — large English-speaking startup base), South Africa (Cape Town, Johannesburg), Nigeria (Lagos), Kenya (Nairobi), Netherlands & Germany (large English-fluent B2B/startup scenes, worth an English-language page even without full localization)
  - **Global English markets:** any English-speaking or English-business-fluent country surfacing meaningful, validated demand in the data above.
- Decide market entry format per country: full localized subfolder (`/dubai/`), English-only landing page, or no dedicated page — based on the validated demand score, not assumption.

### 0.3 Keyword, Topic & Entity Discovery
- Start broad, then expand systematically: seed keywords → Google/Bing autosuggest → "People Also Ask" and "related searches" → AlsoAsked-style question maps → competitor keyword gap (Ahrefs/Semrush) → SERP feature audit (which queries trigger AI Overviews, local packs, image packs, video packs, shopping).
- Build **topical entity maps**, not just keyword lists: for "brand identity," map related entities (logo, visual identity, brand guidelines, brand strategy, brand positioning) so both classic search and AI answer engines can understand topical authority.
- Classify every keyword by intent (informational, commercial-investigation, transactional, navigational) and by funnel stage, so content and CTAs match.
- Discover question-based and conversational queries specifically — these map directly to how people prompt AI assistants (e.g., "how much does a rebrand cost for a Series A startup" vs. just "rebranding agency").

### 0.4 Competitor & SERP Discovery
- Identify true competitors three ways: classic SERP competitors, AI-answer competitors (who ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini/Google AI Overviews currently cite or recommend for your target prompts), and directory-listing competitors (who ranks top on Clutch/DesignRush/GoodFirms/The Manifest for your services).
- Reverse-engineer their content gaps, backlink sources, service-page structure, and schema usage.

### 0.5 Content Gap Discovery
- Cross-reference the keyword/entity map against existing site content to find: missing pages, thin pages, outdated pages, and cannibalizing duplicates.
- Identify content formats competitors and AI engines favor for each topic (comparison tables, calculators, case studies, definitional guides) that you don't yet have.

### 0.6 Technical & Crawl Discovery
- Pull server log files to see what Googlebot, Bingbot, and AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended) actually request versus what's blocked or wasted on low-value URLs.
- Audit crawl budget, orphaned pages, redirect chains, and JavaScript-rendered content that crawlers may not fully see.

### 0.7 AI Answer Engine Discovery ("Share of Model" audit)
- Run your priority buyer prompts (e.g., "best branding agency for a Dubai startup," "who should I hire to rebrand my SaaS company") through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews/AI Mode. Log whether and how **Astrells** is mentioned versus competitors, and in what context (recommended outright, listed among options, or absent entirely).
- Also run direct entity-resolution prompts — "who is Astrells," "is Astrells a legitimate branding agency," "Astrells reviews" — to see whether AI systems currently describe the brand accurately, confuse it with another company, or have no information at all. Gaps here point directly to entity/schema and About-page work in Part 7 and Part 8.
- This is the AI-era equivalent of a rank tracker — treat "citation share" as a KPI (see Reporting, Part 11).

### 0.8 Visual, Video & Social Search Discovery
- Creative agencies are discovered visually as much as textually. Audit and grow presence on: Pinterest (huge for branding/packaging/interior-adjacent visual search), Behance and Dribbble (portfolio discovery + backlinks), Instagram and TikTok (increasingly used as search engines for "who designed this"), YouTube (process videos, case-study walkthroughs, tutorials), and Google Images/Lens.
- Identify which visual assets (logo reveals, packaging mockups, before/after rebrands) have the strongest standalone discovery potential and are currently unindexed or poorly tagged.

### 0.9 Local & Directory Discovery
- Beyond Google Business Profile, map presence and ranking on the directories and award platforms that function as trust-and-discovery layers for creative buyers: Clutch, DesignRush, GoodFirms, The Manifest, Awwwards, CSS Design Awards, and relevant local chambers of commerce or startup hubs per target market.
- Include Bing Places and Apple Business Connect for local/map discovery outside the Google ecosystem.

### 0.10 Voice & Conversational Discovery
- Audit how target queries phrase naturally when spoken ("find me a branding agency near me that works with startups") and ensure FAQ/content structure answers these directly and concisely.

**Output of Part 0:** a living discovery dossier (market scorecard, keyword/entity map, competitor matrix, content gap list, AI-citation baseline, directory-presence audit) that every other part of this system references and updates on a recurring cadence (recommend quarterly, or monthly for AI-citation tracking given how fast that landscape moves).

## PART 6 — AI ANSWER ENGINE OPTIMIZATION (AEO/GEO)

Classic SEO gets you into Google's blue links. **Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)** and **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** get you cited, quoted, or recommended inside AI-generated answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and Google AI Overviews/AI Mode. Treat this as a required layer, not an optional add-on — a growing share of buyer research now starts and sometimes ends inside an AI conversation rather than a traditional search results page.

**Core principles:**

1. **Answer the real question first.** Structure content around the actual questions buyers ask (mined in Part 0.1/0.3), not just keywords.
2. **Be quotable, not just readable.** Each meaningful passage/section should stand alone as a self-contained, verifiable answer — because AI systems often lift short excerpts out of context rather than sending readers to the full page.
3. **Front-load the definition.** Open key sections with the clear, entity-first sentence pattern described in Part 2 — this is the highest-weighted span for AI summarization.
4. **Be evidence-dense.** Favor specific facts, named frameworks, real statistics, and expert quotes over generic claims; generative engines and their retrieval systems favor factual density over keyword density.
5. **Structure for extraction.** Use FAQ sections phrased as real prompts, comparison/scorecard tables, and clear numbered steps or lists wherever the topic supports it.
6. **Keep it current.** AI citation pools favor recently updated content and can deprioritize stale pages — build a refresh cadence into the content calendar rather than a "publish and forget" model.
7. **Build entity authority.** Consistent Organization/Person schema, a coherent "About/Team" presence, and third-party mentions all help AI systems resolve who you are and whether to trust you as a source.

**Measurement:** track "citation share" / "share of model" the same way you'd track rank position — periodically run priority buyer prompts through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews and log whether/how the brand appears versus competitors (see Part 0.7 and Part 11).

### 7A — The AI Crawler Access Layer (robots.txt + llms.txt)

This is now a distinct technical layer separate from classic robots.txt hygiene, because AI companies run **separate bots for training versus real-time retrieval**:

| Purpose | Example crawlers |
|---|---|
| Model training (feeds the model's knowledge, not the live answer) | GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Bytespider, anthropic-ai |
| Real-time retrieval/citation (powers live AI answers, drives referral traffic) | OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot / Claude-User, PerplexityBot, Applebot-Extended |

Recommended default posture for a lead-generation business (visibility-maximizing): explicitly **allow** both training and retrieval bots in robots.txt unless there's a specific proprietary-content reason to block training crawlers — a large share of sites accidentally block one or more of these bots and measurably lose AI-driven citations and referral traffic as a result. Review and set this deliberately rather than relying on a default `User-agent: *` block.

Also publish and maintain an **`llms.txt`** file at the site root: a concise, markdown-style index that states clearly what Astrells is and does, then points AI systems to your most important pages (services, locations, pricing/positioning, case studies). Adoption and actual usage by major AI crawlers is still uneven and evolving, so treat it as a low-cost, forward-looking addition rather than a guaranteed traffic lever — pair it with strong on-page structure and schema, which matter more today.

Monitor AI bot traffic via server/CDN logs (e.g., Cloudflare bot analytics) to see which AI crawlers are actually visiting, and cross-check that against your Part 0.7 citation audit to see whether access is translating into citations.

## FINAL RULES

- Never use black-hat SEO tactics of any kind.
- Never create thin, duplicate, or auto-generated low-value content — including location pages that only swap a city name.
- Never keyword-stuff, and never keyword-stuff prompts either (write for the real question, not the term).
- Never fabricate reviews, testimonials, credentials, or statistics to appear more citable to AI systems.
- Never present different factual content to AI crawlers than to human visitors.
- Always validate a market or content topic with real demand data (Part 0) before building for it.
- Always prioritize, in order: (1) search/user intent, (2) user experience, (3) business conversions, (4) long-term authority — across every discovery channel, not just classic search.

**Your objective is not just ranking. It's making this creative agency effortless to discover — however, wherever, and on whatever platform a buyer starts looking.**
