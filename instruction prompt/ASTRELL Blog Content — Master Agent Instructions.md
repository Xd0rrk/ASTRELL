## Part 4: Your Operating Process

### Phase 0 — Independent Verification Audit (do this first, for all 55, before deep-diving any one)

For every article, using your own research capability:
1. **Confirm or overturn the Part 3 prior take.** Check real search demand, current SERP
   competition, and business fit against Part 1. Don't rubber-stamp the prior pass — it was a
   lighter-touch check without volume data.
2. **Decide KEEP / IMPROVE / REPLACE.**
   - KEEP: real demand, fits a pillar, defensible angle — rebuild in place under the existing title.
   - IMPROVE: right general topic, but the title/angle needs sharpening (merge, retitle, narrow,
     or broaden) before proceeding.
   - REPLACE: fails on demand, fit, or opportunity, or duplicates another slot. Propose 1–3
     replacement topics with real demand and a clear pillar/conversion fit; pick the strongest.
     Record: `Old: "[title]" → New: "[title]" — Reason: [...]`.
3. **Produce one consolidated audit table** (all 55 rows: title, pillar, decision, reasoning,
   replacement title if applicable) as your first deliverable.

**Stop here and surface the table for human review before proceeding to Phase 1 on any individual
article.** Given the acknowledged uncertainty about what's actually valuable here, this checkpoint
exists specifically so a human can sanity-check your direction — especially every REPLACE call and
your resolution of the packaging/spatial-branding question — before you spend real research and
writing effort on 55 full articles.

### Phase 1 — Deep Research (per article, after audit sign-off)

Produce a research brief per article:
- **Search intent** — what the reader wants to know or do by the end.
- **SERP analysis** — top-ranking results for the topic; shared "table stakes" structure; what
  they leave thin, generic, or outdated (your opportunity).
- **Keyword set** — primary keyword, 5–10 secondary/semantic keywords, long-tail variants,
  question-based queries (FAQ candidates).
- **Source gathering** — 3–6 credible sources for factual claims. For Pillar B, prioritize primary
  sources (the AI vendor's own docs/release notes) over secondhand summaries — this space moves
  fast and secondhand content goes stale quickly.
- **Gap opportunity** — the specific way this article will beat what currently ranks.
- **Conversion angle** — how this article connects to an Astrell service or offering.

Do not begin drafting until this brief exists.

### Phase 2 — Content Architecture

- SEO title (primary keyword near the front, under ~60 characters).
- Meta description (under ~155 characters, states the specific value/answer).
- URL slug.
- H1/H2/H3 outline covering table stakes, the gap-opportunity addition, and question-based queries.
- Featured snippet target (paragraph/list/table).
- Internal linking plan — to/from the 6 completed articles and other of the 55 in the same
  cluster, plus the relevant Astrell service page or Pillar B bridge mention.

### Phase 3 — Writing

**Structure:** intent-answering intro (2–3 sentences) → body per outline with real examples/steps
→ "common mistakes" section where relevant → FAQ from real question-based queries → conclusion
with one clear next step (often the Astrell tie-in).

**E-E-A-T:** attribute specific facts to real sources; never invent a statistic, study, quote, or
credential. For Pillar B, be precise about which AI tool/version a technique applies to.

**Quality:** vary sentence structure; avoid stock AI filler phrasing ("in today's fast-paced
world," "it's important to note," "unlock the power of," etc.); match the tone/depth of the 6
completed articles; no keyword stuffing; the Astrell tie-in reads as a natural next step, never an
ad.

### Phase 4 — On-Page SEO Checklist

- [ ] Primary keyword in title, H1, first 100 words, and one H2
- [ ] Secondary/semantic keywords distributed naturally
- [ ] Meta description within length
- [ ] Logical header hierarchy
- [ ] FAQ targets real question-based queries
- [ ] Internal link in and out, including one relevant existing page
- [ ] Exactly one contextually-earned Astrell tie-in/CTA
- [ ] Schema markup recommendation (Article; FAQPage; HowTo if step-based)
- [ ] Descriptive, non-stuffed image alt text
- [ ] No keyword overlap with another of the 61 total articles
- [ ] Readability appropriate for the audience (business owners/marketers for Pillar A, creatives + AI-curious readers for Pillar B)

### Phase 5 — Visual Content

If you have image-generation/sourcing capability: produce images specific to the article's actual
content, with descriptive alt text. If not: produce an image brief per visual (placement, what it
depicts, why it helps, suggested alt text) instead of claiming a finished asset. Astrell is a
design agency — its own visuals need to be held to a high bar; generic stock imagery undercuts
credibility more here than on a typical blog.

### Phase 6 — Quality Assurance (per article, before sign-off)

1. Fact-check every specific claim against the research source list.
2. Originality check — written fresh from research, not reworded from one competitor.
3. Full Phase 4 checklist verified.
4. Cross-article check — no keyword collision with another of the 61.
5. Voice/consistency check against the 6 completed articles.
6. Conversion check — is the next step obvious to the reader?

### Phase 7 — Deliverable Format

**After Phase 0:** the full 55-row audit table, for human review before any writing begins.

**Per article, after sign-off:**
1. Research brief.
2. Old→new record, if replaced.
3. Final article copy (title, meta description, slug, pillar tag A/B).
4. Internal linking notes.
5. Schema markup recommendation (JSON-LD).
6. Image briefs or final image files.

## Part 5: Operating Principles (work efficiently, not just thoroughly)

- **Batch your effort.** Don't spend the same research depth on an obvious, well-established KEEP
  topic as on a genuinely uncertain REPLACE decision. Scale research effort to how uncertain or
  high-stakes the call actually is.
- **Checkpoint before scaling up.** The Phase 0 audit table is a hard stop — get it reviewed before
  committing to 55 full research-and-write cycles.
- **Don't guess when you can verify, and don't over-verify the obvious.** If a topic is a clearly
  established, high-confidence search category (e.g., "web accessibility compliance"), a light
  confirmation is enough — spend your research budget on the ambiguous calls (fit questions,
  REPLACE candidates, new Pillar B topics).
- **Flag uncertainty explicitly rather than resolve it silently.** If you can't confirm whether
  Astrell offers packaging or spatial-branding services, say so in the audit table rather than
  guessing either way.
- **Process in the batch order given** (01 → 11) unless you have a specific, stated reason to
  reprioritize (e.g., Batch 09's AI-search-visibility topics are time-sensitive and may deserve
  earlier placement).

## Part 6: Definition of Done

An article — original, improved, or fully replaced — is complete only when it: fully answers the
identified search intent, covers the SERP table-stakes plus a genuine gap-opportunity addition,
passes the full SEO checklist, contains no unsourced specific claims, matches the site's voice,
includes working internal links and a natural Astrell tie-in, and clearly belongs to Pillar A or
B. Ranking without a plausible path to a lead is not the goal; a lead-generating article that also
ranks well is. Preserving an existing title or topic is never itself a goal — producing something
genuinely worth a reader's time and Astrell's brand is.
